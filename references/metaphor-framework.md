# origami-blue — the metaphor framework

How to turn a transcript into a sequence of visuals that *explain* the idea, not decorate it. Use this for
every new episode before writing any code. The aim is not to copy the last episode — it's to think the way the
last episodes were thought, and arrive somewhere new.

## 1. The principle: show the mechanism

A good origami-blue visual answers *how* or *why*, not *what*. "Feels expensive" as a yellow word is a label.
A balance scale where PRICE drops into one pan and the VALUE pan is empty — that's the mechanism: the price
feels heavy *because nothing sits on the other side*. Viewers with sound off should still get the point.

A metaphor earns its place when it has **built-in motion that matches the sentence** — something tips, fills,
falls, focuses, amplifies, splits, lands. Static icons don't. If the object can't change state on a word, pick
another object.

## 2. Read for the turn

Every episode in this format flips one belief. Find it before anything else, in one sentence:

- ep.03: *Your price isn't the problem — they can't see the value.*
- ep.04: *You undercharge because you don't believe your expertise is enough.*
- ep.05: *You don't have a lead problem — you have an offer problem.*
- ep.06: *Nobody books your 1:1 because your approach isn't clear.*

The turn decides: the hook headline (state it, or state the false belief so the episode can flip it), the one
diagram that carries the episode (built around the turn's mechanism), the payoff (the same diagram resolved),
and the ghost words (the turn's key noun).

Then segment the transcript into beats. Typical spine (≈ 2 min):

| beat | job | typical treatment |
|---|---|---|
| hook (0–6s) | stop the scroll with the turn | kicker + yellow headline + brush underline + ghost word; punch on the key word |
| series intro | identity | tile + ₹1 crore line (§3) |
| setup / symptom | "you think X is the issue" | the episode's main diagram in its *broken* state |
| the turn | "actually it's Y" | strike-through / reframe rows; hero yellow; hard punch |
| evidence | why Y | diagram performs the cause (tip, gap, noise, chain) |
| example | a concrete case | full-page cutaway (B-roll page) or label rows |
| consequence | what goes wrong | the broken state made visible (falling dominoes, empty slots, blurred chart) |
| instruction | what to do | ordered structure (stack, IF/THEN, two-pill chain, check rows) |
| payoff | the resolution | **callback**: same diagram, resolved, turning yellow |
| CTA | comment keyword | key-caps |

## 3. The opening: hook, series, one-offs

**Hook (frame 1).** The title is on screen from the first frame — never wait for the sentence to finish. Two
proven shapes — pick by what the opening sentence does: if it states a belief the episode will flip, state the
turn contrarian-style; if it describes a pain the viewer feels, name the pain (then the turn lands later as its
own beat):
- *Contrarian statement of the turn*: YOUR PRICE ISN'T / **THE PROBLEM.** — YOU DON'T HAVE A / **LEAD PROBLEM.**
- *Name the pain*: WHY YOU KEEP / **UNDERCHARGING.** — NOT ENOUGH PEOPLE ON YOUR / **1:1 CALLS.**
Kicker small and white (the setup), headline big and yellow (the noun that hurts), a blue dry-brush underline
that paints on when the key word is spoken, a ghost of the core noun behind the head (LEADS, PRICE, 1:1).

**Series.** A series is recognised by exact repetition of its frame, not by novelty:
- tile on "Welcome to …": same layout, only the episode number changes; number rises, words land on their
  spoken words, tile wipes up;
- the mission line right after (WHAT IT TAKES TO BUILD A ₹1 CRORE coaching business.);
- same CTA format.
For a **new series**: design once — series name in two weights, a number treatment (big `ep. 0N`), a one-line
footer promise, one accent bar in blue — then never change it.

**One-off (no series).** Skip the tile. Let the hook hold slightly longer and add a one-line "promise" kicker
under it (what you'll learn), or go straight into the first diagram while the hook headline is still exiting.

## 4. The fold method — six questions per beat

For each beat, answer in order. Write the answers into the beat sheet (cards.json "visual" and "motion").

1. **What is the shape of this idea?** Reduce the sentence to a verb: compare, weigh, measure a gap, order,
   filter/qualify, cause→effect, amplify, hide/reveal, focus/blur, choose a path, fill capacity, grow,
   transform A→B, cover all areas.
2. **Which physical thing already does that verb?** (§5–6) Prefer objects everyone has touched: scales,
   sliders, dials, dominoes, stairs, bridges, lenses, eye charts, funnels, sieves, slots, roads, icebergs,
   magnets, levers, hourglasses. Line art only — never photos, clip-art or emoji.
3. **What changes, on which word?** Map each state change to a spoken word (from the word-level transcript).
   A diagram with 3–4 states over 10–15s is ideal: appear → broken state → reveal → (later) resolution.
4. **Where is the yellow — if any?** At most one hero: the payoff word, the resolved part, the thing to change
   (or the pain noun in a hook headline). A broken-state diagram may have none; structure is blue.
5. **Behind or in front?** Line-art objects that can be partly hidden by hair → `.behind` (chroma depth: the
   speaker stands in front of the idea). Text that must be read → `.front`. Ghost words → behind.
6. **Can it come back?** Plan 1–2 callbacks per episode: the same diagram returns later, resolved (the scale
   returns tipped the other way; the slots fill; the questions get ticks; the blurred chart snaps sharp as
   "CLEAR OFFER." racks into focus). Callbacks make a 2-minute reel feel designed, not decorated.

Then ask the hard question: *is this a metaphor, or just a label in a box?* If a box with words would say the
same thing, keep thinking — or deliberately use the label-column grammar (it's honest structure, not a fake
metaphor) and save metaphors for the ideas that need them.

## 5. Visual families already used (don't repeat across episodes)

"Don't repeat" means don't reuse the **object** (another balance scale, another slider, another life wheel).
A verb may recur with a different object — cause→effect was dominoes in ep.06; a fishbone, a chain reaction of
gears, or a fuse would be fine later. Be wary of objects that *look* like an old one (concentric target rings
vs the life wheel): change the silhouette, not just the label.

| family | object | performance | used in |
|---|---|---|---|
| weigh / compare | balance scale PRICE vs VALUE | weight drops, beam tips, empty pan lights up; returns tipped | ep.03 |
| journey | A → B bridge, dot travels | arcs draw, "your consulting" pill rides the bridge | ep.03 |

## 6. Fresh metaphor bank (unused — pick, adapt, or invent better)

| idea | object | performance |
|---|---|---|
| leaky funnel / retention | bucket with holes | water (halftone) pours in, drips out; plugging holes raises the level |
| positioning / niche | focus ring / zoom lens | wide blurry crowd → zoom ring turns → one sharp figure |
| trust built over time | bridge planks | planks lay one by one on each proof point; the last one lets a dot cross |
| authority | stacked bricks / column | each credential is a brick; the column reaches a "trust" line |
| leverage | lever and fulcrum | small weight + long lever lifts a big block; fulcrum slides on "system" |
| compounding | snowball down a slope | ball grows with each roll; numbers stamp nothing — size says it |
| focus vs scatter | dartboard | darts spread vs grouped on bullseye (no cartoon darts — clean line marks) |
| bottleneck | hourglass | sand jams at the neck labelled with the constraint; widening it releases flow |
| audience attraction | magnet field lines | filings align toward a clear message, scatter around a vague one |
| objections | shield / umbrella | objections rain as small labels, deflect off a clear promise |
| pricing tiers / value ladder | staircase | figure climbs; each step = offer tier; yellow top step |
| consistency | streak calendar grid | cells fill in a rhythm; a missing week breaks the line |
| signal vs noise (content) | radio dial | tuning needle sweeps static until one station locks clear |
| decision fatigue | branching tree | too many branches greyed out; one path highlighted |
| hidden cost | receipt tape (line-art, no logos) | line items print; the last unexpected item turns yellow |
| speed vs direction | compass + speedometer | needle spins fast but compass points wrong; compass corrects |
| problem/solution fit | puzzle pieces | mismatched tab doesn't fit; reshaped tab clicks in |
| transformation | before/after split frame (line art, same scene twice) | divider wipes across; the "after" side turns yellow |
| packaging an idea | folding a sheet into a box (origami!) | flat sheet with labels folds into a cube labelled OFFER |
| clarity of offer | lighthouse beam | dim beam vs bright beam cutting fog to a single boat |

Rules for the bank: line art + one fill; nothing cute, no faces on objects, no logos or real brands, no fake
documents passed off as real. The origami fold itself is a signature move worth using once per series for a
"packaging" idea.

## 7. Episode rhythm and arc

- **Alternate grammars.** Never two label-row beats in a row; alternate kinetic headline → diagram → rows →
  cutaway → callback. Pattern of ep.05: hook (headline) → tile → ₹1 crore → eyes (diagram, behind) →
  reframe (rows) → eye chart (page) → tally (rows) → amplifier (diagram, behind) → stack (rows) → choice (pills)
  → tally callback → STOP (headline) → choice callback → CLEAR OFFER (rack focus) → CTA.
- **Breathing room.** Leave 2–6s stretches with only the speaker and subtitles between dense beats; graphics
  should feel placed, not wallpapered. The reference episodes have a graphic up ~75–85% of the runtime with
  3–6 speaker-only breaths; avoid more than ~25s of back-to-back diagrams without a breath or a headline beat.
- **One full-page cutaway** per episode, at the example or the deepest idea, 4–7s.
- **Camera follows meaning**: wide while diagrams are up, punch on claims, literal moves when the words invite
  them ("step back" → pull out, "stop" → hard punch).
- **Ghost words** on 3 hero beats max (hook noun, the turn's noun, payoff/CTA noun).
- **End on the resolved callback, then the CTA** — the last idea seen should be the fixed version of the first
  problem. If the script puts the payoff before a final instruction ("so before you post tomorrow, ask…"),
  follow the script: resolve the callback where it's said, make the instruction its own short beat (often a
  callback used as a test), then the CTA. Never reorder the speaker's argument.

## 8. Writing the words on screen

- Use the speaker's words wherever possible; compress to the noun phrase ("THE LIFE AFTER", "YOUR APPROACH").
- Interpretive labels are allowed when they name the mechanism in 1–3 words ("THE GAP", "AMPLIFIED",
  "BUILD ORDER") — never slogans the speaker didn't imply.
- Kicker 2–6 words; headline 1–3 words; serif notes ≤ 5 words with a full stop ("not selling more.").
- Questions keep their "?" in blue; payoff lines end with a full stop.

## 9. Litmus tests before the cards

- Sound off: can you follow the episode from the cards alone?
- At most one yellow per beat, on the thing to remember? Blue only for structure?
- Does every element enter on a spoken word (write the word in the motion notes)?
- Is any visual just a box with a sentence? Replace or justify.
- Any reused diagram from a previous episode? Replace.
- Is there a callback? Is there one cutaway? Do the ghost words add depth, not clutter?
- Would a premium editorial magazine run this frame? Would a template site sell it? (Second answer must be no.)
- Safe zones clear; nothing below y 1380 except captions; nothing visible before its word.

## 10. Worked examples

See `examples/README.md` for the full beat tables. Condensed reasoning for two of them:

**ep.05 "lead problem → offer problem".** Turn: attention isn't the bottleneck, clarity is. So the main
diagram had to show *attention arriving and failing to convert*: eyes with sight-lines to a dim YOUR OFFER box
(attention ✓, conversion ✗). Clarity got its own physical metaphor — an eye chart that defocuses ("not very
clear to them") — which pays off at the end when CLEAR OFFER. racks from blur to sharp. Paid ads became an
amplifier because the speaker's point is that ads only make louder what's already there: clean wave in → big
clean wave; untested offer in → big noise. "Work on your offer before lead-gen" is about *order*, so a stack
whose upper blocks wobble until the OFFER foundation slides in. "Take a step back" got a literal camera
pull-back.

**ep.06 "nobody books your 1:1".** Turn: the approach is invisible, so people can't qualify themselves. The
symptom is capacity → seven call slots, one filled. Self-qualification is literally the questions buyers ask →
question rows with blue "?" (callback: ticks). The diabetes example is a choice between methods → three
hand-painted roads on a paper page. "Unclear approach → unclear offer → wrong people" is a chain → dominoes
tipping on each word, the last one falling flat. Payoff: every slot fills and turns yellow on "higher
transformation".
